# Got an email from a "Shopify expert"? How to check it before you reply

New Shopify stores get cold offers within a day. How to tell a real partner from a scam, share access safely, and where to report one.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 25 November 2026 12 min read

Treat it as unverified until you have checked it, and never reply with your login. Shopify says it never asks for sensitive information such as passwords by email, and its real emails come from shopify.com addresses, never Gmail. A real Shopify Partner can be found in Shopify's own Partner Directory and will ask for access through a collaborator request that you approve, with only the permissions the job needs. Nobody needs your password, an upfront fee to a stranger or a "code" you must buy before your store can sell. We read Shopify's help centre, the regulators' pages in five countries and nine Shopify Community threads from store owners who got these messages, on 16 November 2026.

Key takeaways

- One new store owner counted more than 10 offers of SEO and website help in under 24 hours after the store went live. Volume like that is common, and it is not a sign your store is broken.
- Shopify sends email only from addresses such as @shopify.com and @email.shopify.com, and says emails from Gmail or Yahoo "aren't from Shopify". Forward fakes to phishing@shopify.com.
- The Partner Directory only lists partners in Shopify's Select tier or above (Silver from 1 January 2027), and since 9 November 2026 its profiles are built mostly from Shopify's own records of their work.
- Give access with the 4-digit collaborator request code and the fewest permissions. Shopify says that if you are not expecting a request, you "shouldn't accept" it.
- Google's own advice: "Be wary of SEO firms" that "email you out of the blue", and "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google."

## Why does a new Shopify store get so many offers?

In one Shopify Community thread, an owner listed a new store and within a day had more than ten messages from people offering SEO and website help. One said they were a Shopify marketing strategist, asked for a small fee by PayPal, offered to work through the store's collaborator code and avoided a video call with an excuse about a seminar ([Shopify Community: "SEO Strategist or scam?"](https://community.shopify.com/t/seo-strategist-or-scam/408148)). Others in the thread said scripts find new stores and send messages that read as if a tool wrote them.

They land when the store has no sales yet. Another owner with no traffic got an email offering to fix "problems" for a fee ([Shopify Community: "website developer"](https://community.shopify.com/t/website-developer/420079)). One reply said some offers are real; another said the aim is a first payment, then rising fees for "fixes" sold as urgent. Both can be true, so you need a way to check, not a rule to ignore everything.

Google's own page on hiring an SEO says "Be wary of SEO firms and web consultants or agencies that email you out of the blue," and quotes a typical opener: "I visited your website and noticed that you are not listed..." ([Google Search Central: Do you need an SEO?](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo)).

## Which messages are the common ones?

The same few approaches came up again and again in the threads we read.

**Common cold offers to Shopify store owners, from Shopify Community threads read on 16 November 2026**

| The message | What it asks for | The check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Shopify team" or "Shopify support" email | A click, a login, ID or payment details | Look at the sender's domain. Shopify does not send from Gmail and does not ask for passwords by email. |
| "Your store has errors" audit | A fee to fix problems they name but do not show | Ask which pages and what exactly is wrong, then check it yourself or ask in the Shopify Community. |
| "Shopify SEO strategist" or "certified expert" | A small fee by PayPal, then your collaborator code | Search the Partner Directory for the business, and ask for a video call before anything else. |
| Commission-only sales help | A share of your sales, and often access to the store | Written terms that say how a sale is counted, and no store access until you have checked who they are. |
| "Purchase code" or upfront activation fee | A payment before your checkout will "work" | No such code exists. Ask Shopify Support through the help centre, not the person who emailed you. |
| "Send me your login" or "add me as staff" | Your email and password, or a staff account | Never share a password. A partner sends a collaborator request with the code you give them. |

In the clearest case, a store owner was told to buy a "code" for 100 to 200 dollars through a freelance marketplace before customers could check out. Everyone who replied said no such code exists, that the Shopify plan already covers checkout, and that anyone who had paid should dispute the charge with the marketplace and their bank ([Shopify Community: "Purchasing Codes"](https://community.shopify.com/t/purchasing-codes/657862)). The owner had paid nothing.

Others were less lucky. One owner says a developer who falsely claimed to be affiliated with Shopify deleted the whole store, and the first advice in the replies was to ask Shopify Support whether it could be recovered ([Shopify Community: "Rebuilding Store"](https://community.shopify.com/t/rebuilding-store/607338)). Another paid for a store with 20 products and found neither had been built ([Shopify Community: "Store was suppose to be put together"](https://community.shopify.com/t/store-was-suppose-to-be-put-together/677298)).

## Is the email really from Shopify?

Shopify's help centre gives the official domains: "@shopify.com", "@email.shopify.com", "@em.shopify.com" and "@shopify-billpay.melio.com". It says emails from public services like Gmail or Yahoo are not from Shopify, and that "Shopify never asks you for sensitive information directly through an email message", with passwords, identification and banking details as examples ([Shopify Help Center: protect your account against phishing](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/account-security/phishing)). Its other signs include urgent or threatening language and links to unfamiliar websites.

Anyone can type "Shopify Support" as their name, so look at the address after the @, and do not use the message's links or phone numbers. The US Federal Trade Commission's advice is "Don't use the contact information in the unexpected message"; contact the company through an address you already know is real ([FTC: how to avoid imposter scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/how-avoid-imposter-scams)). For Shopify, that means typing the admin address yourself and opening Support from the help centre.

Fake Shopify emails are a cousin of the fake client briefs that reach agencies. Our case studies of the [Quatgio scam email](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/quatgio-scam-email) and the [Fiverr fake order scam](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/fiverr-fake-order-scam) show where the links in those messages actually lead.

## How do you check that someone is a real Shopify Partner?

Start with Shopify's [Partner Directory](https://www.shopify.com/partners/directory). Shopify says it "lists all established Shopify agency partners" and that merchants can contact them directly ([Shopify Help Center: how the Partner Directory works](https://help.shopify.com/en/partners/directory/how-it-works)). Eligibility depends on tier: "Partners in the Select tier or above are invited" to join, and the same page says that from 1 January 2027 partners are eligible at Silver tier ([Shopify Help Center: eligibility for the Partner Directory](https://help.shopify.com/en/partners/grow-your-business/partner-directory/join)).

A profile shows the partner's location, estimated team size, how many shops they work with now, how long they have been a Shopify Partner and their top services over the last 4 years ([Shopify Help Center: hiring and working with Shopify Partners](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/partner-directory/hiring-help)). On 9 November 2026 Shopify redesigned the directory: profiles are now built mostly from the partner's collaboration and store transfer history over the past four years, Shopify sets the tier, tenure and top services, and reviews and ratings were removed from profile pages ([Shopify developer changelog, 9 November 2026](https://shopify.dev/changelog/partner-directory-profiles-now-display-verified-collaboration-data)). The partner still writes its own name, description and contact details, and picks its featured clients.

So the check works in one direction. If the business that emailed you has a profile, contact it through that profile, not the email, and confirm it is the same people. No profile is not proof of fraud, since many honest freelancers are too small or too new to be listed, but then they must show you more. Two warnings from the threads: one owner says a developer shown as "certified" turned out to be a scam ([Shopify Community: Shopify and Paddle integration](https://community.shopify.com/t/where-can-i-find-a-developer-for-a-shopify-paddle-3rd-part-gateway-integration/577845)), and another learned that a high place on the community leaderboard "simply means active on the forum board" ([Shopify Community: warning to other merchants](https://community.shopify.com/t/warning-to-other-merchants-my-experience-with-a-leaderboard-member/582622)).

Shopify also says "the Partner will bill you directly", and those charges do not show on your Shopify bill. Shopify does not hold your money in between, so your protection is the written agreement and whatever your card or payment platform offers.

## What can a real partner show you?

Before you pay or grant access, anyone who wants to work on your store should be able to give you:

- A business name and website you can look up yourself, and, if they claim to be a Shopify Partner, their Partner Directory profile.
- A short video call. In the SEO thread, the "strategist" would not do one.
- Live stores they have worked on that you can open, and what they did on each. Google suggests asking an SEO to "show me examples of your previous work".
- What exactly is wrong with your store, page by page, before any fee.
- A written quote with the scope, the price, the timeline and what you will own at the end.
- A collaborator request sent with the code you give them, not a request for your password.

For store builds, our look at [what Shopify partners charge](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/shopify-development-india) uses the directory's own profiles.

## Should you accept a commission-only sales offer?

Commission sounds safe, because you only pay when you sell. In one thread, an owner selling art prints had received many emails offering to grow sales for a share, and only one mentioned problems the owner had already noticed ([Shopify Community: "3rd party help or mo"](https://community.shopify.com/t/3rd-party-help-or-mo/707179)). The replies: ask what they would change and why, and for past work; avoid anyone who promises guaranteed sales or rankings; get the commission and how a sale is counted in writing. Someone who can tidy your store pages may not be able to bring you customers, and the other way round.

Watch for the access request inside the offer. "Add me as staff so I can set up tracking" gives a stranger your store before you know who they are. A screenshot or a product link usually shows them enough; real work goes through a collaborator account, as below.

## How do you get help without handing over your store?

Shopify built collaborator accounts for this. You copy a 4-digit collaborator request code from the Security section of your Users page and give it only to the partner you have chosen; "Only the Partners who you share the code with can request access to your store." A new code stops the old ones working, and Shopify's advice on an unexpected request is plain: "If you aren't expecting a request, or if you don't recognize the sender, then you shouldn't accept their request" ([Shopify Help Center: collaborator accounts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/security/collaborator-accounts)).

Give the fewest permissions the job needs. Shopify marks some permissions as sensitive, such as managing payment settings, and some things stay with the store owner whatever you grant: managing Shopify Payments, deactivating the store and "Transferring or changing ownership" ([Shopify Help Center: store permissions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/permissions/store-permissions); [roles managed by Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/roles-managed-by-shopify)). Two small habits help as well:

- Before anyone edits your theme, duplicate it. Shopify calls a copy a backup "so that you can discard your changes" ([Shopify Help Center: duplicating themes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/managing-themes/duplicating-themes)).
- Know which password someone is asking for. The password that lets visitors see a store in private mode is not your admin login, and Shopify says "Don't use the same password" for both ([Shopify Help Center: password page](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/password-page)). One lets someone look at the store; the other gives them the business.

Our [guide to giving an agency access safely](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/agency-access-guide) covers Shopify, Google and Meta accounts step by step, including what to remove when the work ends.

## What if you already gave access or paid?

Act today, in this order:

1. Remove them in Settings, then Users. Removing a collaborator is permanent; to return they must send a new request. Remove any other account you do not recognise, here and anywhere else they could reach (our [access guide](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/agency-access-guide) covers Google and Meta).
2. Secure your own login. Shopify's advice after a phishing click is to change your password, turn on two-step authentication and contact Shopify Support to check for access you did not allow.
3. Check installed apps, payment and payout settings and the theme. A backup copy of the theme lets you go back.
4. Call your bank or card provider if you paid. Australia's Scamwatch says to "tell them to stop any transactions", and to act fast ([Scamwatch: report a scam](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam)). If you paid through a freelance marketplace or PayPal, open a dispute there too.
5. Save the emails, chats and receipts before you block anyone.
6. Report the person to Shopify. Forward fake Shopify emails to phishing@shopify.com. If someone who is a Shopify Partner broke the rules, Shopify asks you to use its [Report a Partner violation form](https://www.shopify.com/legal/tools/report-an-issue/report-a-partner-violation) ([Shopify Help Center: working with Shopify Partners](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/working_with_Shopify_Partners)).
7. Report it in your country, as below. If you lost a lot of money or the store, check with a lawyer about getting it back.

## Where do you report it in your country?

In the US, the FTC's Impersonation Rule, effective 1 April 2024, makes it an unfair or deceptive practice to "materially misrepresent" an "affiliation with, including endorsement or sponsorship by," a business ([Federal Register: Trade Regulation Rule on Impersonation of Government and Businesses](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-03-01/html/2024-04335.htm)). Whether it covers your case is a question for a lawyer; the report itself is free.

**Official reporting routes, from each government's own pages, read on 16 November 2026**

| Country | Suspicious email | If you lost money |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States | Report it to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) | The same report, and call your bank or card provider |
| United Kingdom | Forward it to report@phishing.gov.uk ([NCSC: report a scam email](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-email)) | England, Wales and Northern Ireland: Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040. Scotland: Police Scotland on 101. |
| Netherlands | The [Fraudehelpdesk](https://www.fraudehelpdesk.nl/), which has a reporting route for business owners (ondernemers) | The same Fraudehelpdesk report, or call it on 088 7867372, and call your bank |
| Australia | [Scamwatch report form](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam) | Your bank first, then a police report through cyber.gov.au; Scamwatch also points to IDCARE for support |
| United Arab Emirates | The Ministry of Interior's eCrimes platform, Dubai Police's eCrime website or Abu Dhabi Police's Aman service ([UAE government portal: cyber safety](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/cyber-safety-and-digital-security)) | The same routes, the federal Public Prosecution's My Safe Society app, or the nearest police station; call 999 for help |

## The 2-minute checklist

Before you answer any message offering to fix, grow or "help" your store:

1. Read the sender's address, not the name. Gmail, Yahoo or a look-alike domain is not Shopify.
2. Do not click its links. Open your Shopify admin by typing the address yourself.
3. Search the Partner Directory for the business. If it is listed, contact it through that profile.
4. Ask what exactly is wrong, on which page. A vague "errors" audit is a sales pitch.
5. Ask for a video call and live examples of their work.
6. Pay nothing before a written quote, and never pay for a "code" to make checkout work.
7. Never share your password. Give the collaborator code only to someone you have chosen, with the fewest permissions.
8. Duplicate your theme before anyone edits it.
9. If something already went wrong, remove their access, change your password, call your bank and report it.
