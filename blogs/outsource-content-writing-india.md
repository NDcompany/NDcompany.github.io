# Outsourcing content writing to India: what 21 agency websites tell a buyer

We read the websites of 27 agencies on Semrush's India content lists. Of the 21 that sell writing, 4 publish a price, 3 mention ownership and none offers a sample, so here is what to ask.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 27 November 2026 9 min read

To outsource content writing to India, you need answers that most agency websites do not give: the price per post, the English they write in, how they use AI and who owns the words once you have paid. On 27 November 2026 we read the websites of 27 agencies on Semrush's directory for content marketing in India. 21 sell content writing. Only 4 of those 21 publish any price, 2 say which English they write, 3 say anything about who owns the content, and none offers a sample piece.

Key takeaways

- 4 of the 21 agencies that sell writing publish a price: $0.02 to $0.06 a word at one, monthly packages from $150 at another, "from $299" a month at a third, and $4,500 to $24,000+ a month at an enterprise firm. None prices a single blog post.
- 8 say something about AI, and they disagree: "100% human written" at one end, AI drafts delivered "with or without humanization" at the other. Ask which you are buying.
- 2 say they write in native or British English. The other 19 leave it to you to ask.
- 3 mention ownership. Two say the client owns the content, and one keeps it and gives the client a licence. 18 say nothing, so put the transfer in writing.
- None offers a free sample or trial piece, and one says outright that it does not. Ask for published work you can check.

This is the writing version of a question we have already looked at for code. If you are weighing India for a website or software as well, start with our guide to [outsourcing web development to India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/outsource-web-development-india).

## How we did the study

We used [Semrush's Agency Partners list for content marketing in India](https://agencies.semrush.com/list/content-marketing/india/) as it showed on 27 November 2026. It has one page of 26 profiles. We left out one profile that belongs to a single consultant, not an agency, which leaves 25. Semrush's [content production list for India](https://agencies.semrush.com/list/content-production/india/) has 17 profiles, and 15 of them were already on the first list, so it added 2. That makes 27 agencies. Two of them show an address outside India on Semrush, but describe themselves as Indian or give an Indian address on their own sites, so we kept them.

For each agency we read its own website only: the content writing, copywriting and blog pages, pricing, FAQs, about pages and terms. We copied each claim word for word with the page it came from, and checked every quote in this post against the live page on the same day. Of the 27, 21 sell content writing, though at two of them it is a single line on a broader marketing page. The other 6 sell branding, software or marketing strategy without writing. The figures below are out of those 21.

The study records what agencies say about themselves. It does not measure how well they write, and an agency that says nothing about ownership may still hand it over without trouble. It covers one directory, not every agency in India. We do not name any agency, and the rows in our data file are numbered, not named.

## How much does content writing from India cost?

The published prices are too few to give a typical rate. Here is all of them:

**Every content writing price published by the 21 agencies, as shown on 27 November 2026**

| Agency | Price as published | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $0.02 to $0.06 a word | Priced by word count, not by package |
| B | $150, $320 and $450 a month | 4, 8 or 16 blog posts of 500 words a month, the same number of 700-word articles, plus guest posts, press releases and short keyword descriptions |
| C | From $299 a month | A custom package; no volumes given |
| D | $4,500 to $8,000, $9,500 to $16,000 and $17,500 to $24,000+ a month | Enterprise B2B content, from 6 to 8 long pieces a month up to 25+ |

At a word rate of $0.02 to $0.06, a 1,500-word blog post would cost $30 to $90 before any extras. That is our arithmetic, not a quote from the agency. The monthly packages are harder to compare, because they bundle different lengths and extras: agency B's plans count 500-word blog posts and 700-word articles together with guest posts and press releases.

When you ask for quotes, ask each agency for the same thing: the price of one post of a stated length, with the research, the editing and the number of revisions spelled out. Then you are comparing like with like.

## Do Indian content agencies use AI?

Some do, some say they do not, and most do not say. 8 of the 21 address it, and their positions run the full range. One says its content is "100% human written" and uses AI only as a reference. Another says it does not use AI to write at all. Another sells two products side by side, an AI-assisted draft edited by a writer and a fully human one. One says AI drafts are used for speed, but you will never receive "raw, unreviewed AI output". And one says AI-generated content "can be delivered with or without humanization".

Google does not object to AI as such. Its [guidance on AI-generated content](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content) says it focuses on the quality of content, not how it is produced, and that using AI mainly to manipulate rankings breaks its spam policies. So the real question is not "AI or no AI". It is who checks the facts, who adds what only your business knows, and whether you are told which process you are paying for. Agree your rule in writing, whatever it is. If your pages also need to be quoted by AI assistants, our guide to [writing content AI search can cite](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/optimize-content-for-llms) covers what helps.

## Will they write US or UK English?

Only 2 of the 21 say. One promises native English-speaking writers, and one has a desk for British English and another for Canadian English. The rest are silent, which does not mean they cannot do it, only that you have to ask.

The difference is more than color or colour. Dates, units, currency, legal terms and even common words differ between US, UK and Australian readers. Name your market in the brief, ask for a style sheet that records the choice, and check the first piece for the small things: date formats, spelling of the same word across pages and whether prices carry the right symbol.

## Who owns the content after you pay?

18 of the 21 agencies do not say. Of the 3 that do, two say the client owns the content (one of them only once every fee is paid), and the third says in its terms that its content stays its own intellectual property "unless otherwise agreed", with a limited licence for the client. One of the two that promise ownership says so on its blog writing page, but its terms of use speak only of web design and code. Read the terms, not only the sales page.

Indian law makes this matter. Under the [Copyright Act, 1957](https://copyright.gov.in/Documents/Copyright_Act_1957.pdf), the agency is normally the first owner of what its employed writers produce (section 17), and a transfer to you has to be in writing (section 19). If the assignment states no period, the Act reads it as five years; if it names no territory, it presumes India. A line such as "all rights pass to the client on payment" can leave a business in London or New York with less than it thinks. We explain the clauses to ask for, and the US and UK rules, in [outsourcing, NDAs and who owns the work](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/outsourcing-nda-ip-ownership). It is not legal advice, so have a lawyer in your own country read the contract.

## Can you see a sample first?

Not from the websites. None of the 21 offers a sample or a trial piece, and one says it will share past work but does "not offer free demos/sample content writing". That is a fair position: a custom sample is real work. But it means you should judge an agency on writing you can check.

Ask for three published pieces in a field close to yours, with the live links. Read one closely: does it answer the question in its title early, are its numbers linked to a source, and would someone in your trade find a mistake? Then commission one paid piece before a monthly plan. One post costs little next to six months of the wrong writer.

## What should you put in writing before you hire?

1. Price per piece and length. The price of one post of a stated word count, and what a month includes.
2. Research and sources. Whether every figure and claim links to where it came from, and who checks them.
3. AI. Whether it may be used, for what, and who edits and checks the result.
4. English variant and style. US, UK or another market, recorded in a style sheet the writers follow.
5. Revisions. How many rounds per piece, and what counts as a revision rather than a rewrite.
6. Delivery dates. When drafts arrive, and what happens when one is late.
7. Originality. A plagiarism check before delivery, and the tool used.
8. Ownership. Which content you own on full payment, for how long and where, signed before work starts.
9. Confidentiality. An NDA if the brief includes anything private.
10. Access. If the agency publishes for you, its own named login on your site, never yours.

If you use an agency for SEO as well as writing, the same questions apply to the rest of the work. We ran similar checks on SEO firms in [how to choose an SEO agency in India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/choose-seo-agency-india).
