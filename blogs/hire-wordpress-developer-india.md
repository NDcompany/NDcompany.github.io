# How do you hire a WordPress developer in India? What 30 agency websites show

We read the websites of 30 Indian web development agencies. Only 4 publish an hourly rate and only 8 say you own the code, so here is what to ask and put in writing.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 7 November 2026 11 min read

To hire a WordPress developer in India, you mostly need to settle things the agencies' own websites leave unsaid: who owns the code, whose name the hosting and licences are in, and what support you get after launch. On 7 November 2026 we read the websites of 30 Indian web development agencies listed on Semrush's agency directory. 22 offer WordPress, only 4 publish an hourly developer rate, and only 8 say that you own the code or site once you have paid. The other 20 say nothing about ownership at all.

Key takeaways

- 22 of the 30 agencies offer WordPress development. The other 8 are mostly SEO or marketing shops with a thin web-build page, so check what a "web development" listing actually sells.
- Only 4 publish an hourly developer rate: from £10 an hour, from US$20, from US$25 with a 200-hour minimum, and $25 to $55 by seniority at a firm that does not offer WordPress. 1 publishes a monthly dedicated-developer rate, from £1,280 a month for 160 hours.
- 8 say the client owns the code or site after payment, 1 keeps custom code unless it is transferred in writing, 1 is unclear and 20 say nothing. Put ownership in the contract.
- 5 state a free support period after launch, from one month to three months. 3 price a maintenance plan, from $45 to $199 a month at the two monthly ones.
- 0 of the 30 claim a WordPress-specific credential. One says "certified WordPress developers" without saying who certifies them, so ask for work you can open and test.

If you are weighing India as a whole, not only WordPress, start with our guide to [outsourcing web development to India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/outsource-web-development-india). This post goes narrower, to the questions that matter when the site runs on WordPress.

## How we did the study

We used the first page of [Semrush's Agency Partners list for web development in India](https://agencies.semrush.com/list/web-development/india/), in the order it showed on 7 November 2026. It listed 37 profiles. We could record 34 of them from the agencies' own websites. Of those 34, 30 are based in India. Four listed agencies were based in the UK, Australia and Canada, so we left them out and kept the sample to Indian firms.

For each agency we read its own site only: web development and WordPress pages, pricing, "hire a developer" pages, maintenance plans, about pages, FAQs and terms. We copied each claim word for word with the page it came from. The study records what agencies say about themselves. It does not measure how good their work is, and an agency that says nothing about code ownership may still hand over the site without trouble. It covers one directory page, not every agency in India. We do not name any agency, and the rows at the end of this post are numbered, not named.

Read the shortest bars first. Four prices an hour, one monthly developer rate and no credentials means that for most agencies you will learn the price and the terms only by asking. That is normal, but it means you should arrive at the call with your own list of what to put in writing, which is the last half of this post.

## What is a WordPress developer's hourly rate in India?

Of the 30 agencies, 4 publish one. The published rates we saw were from £10 an hour, from US$20, from US$25 with a 200-hour minimum, and $25 to $55 by seniority. That last one belongs to a firm that does not offer WordPress, so it says something about developers in general, not WordPress work.

Three of four are "from" prices, which means the entry-level developer, not the person who would lead your project. A minimum of 200 hours is a real commitment, and it moves the number you pay far more than the hourly figure does. Ask which developer level the rate is for, whether there is a minimum, and what counts as billable: calls, project management, rework after a bug you reported.

One of the 30 publishes a monthly rate for a dedicated developer: from £1,280 a month for 160 hours. Notice what a monthly rate buys. You pay for the hours whether your queue is full or empty. For a steady stream of small changes that suits you. For one build with a date at the end, a fixed scope set in writing is usually clearer. We covered what whole websites cost in [what Indian agencies charge for a website](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/agency-prices-india), and you can test a rough figure for your own project in our [website cost calculator](https://ndcompany.in/tools/website-cost-calculator).

## Should you hire a freelancer or an agency?

It depends on what happens when the one person is ill, busy or gone. A freelancer is usually cheaper per hour and you talk to the person who writes the code. An agency costs more per hour and adds a project lead, a second developer who can read the first one's work, and a company you can contract with. Our data cannot tell you which is better, because we only read agencies. It does show what agencies sell as their edge: 7 of the 30 claim to work in or overlap your hours, 10 mention an NDA and 5 give a free support period. None of these is hard for a careful freelancer to offer too, so ask any freelancer the same questions and expect the same answers in writing.

A reasonable rule: for a small brochure site with one owner who can answer questions quickly, a freelancer with work you can open and test is fine. For a store, a site that several people edit or anything tied to your sales, you want a contract with a firm that has more than one person who knows your site.

## When does a dedicated WordPress developer make sense?

When you have enough steady work to fill the month, such as new landing pages every week, content changes, plugin updates and fixes. A dedicated developer works only on your site for an agreed number of hours, which is what the single published monthly rate in our sample offers: 160 hours a month.

If you cannot fill 160 hours, ask about a smaller block of hours, or about a maintenance plan. Maintenance is the cheaper way to keep a finished site healthy. Only 3 agencies price one. Two list monthly plans from $45 to $199 a month, and one sells 3, 6 or 12-month lump sums from $249. Ask what a plan covers in plain terms: how often core, theme and plugin updates are applied, whether backups are included and where they are stored, how many hours of changes are in the price, and how fast a broken page gets fixed.

## What about a WooCommerce developer in India?

WooCommerce is the free plugin that turns a WordPress site into a store, so everything above applies, with more at stake because the site now takes orders. We did not count WooCommerce separately in this study, so we cannot say how many of the 30 do it well. What we can tell you is what to ask before you hire someone for a store.

- Which payment gateways have they set up for buyers in your country, and who holds the gateway account? It should be yours.
- Who pays for paid extensions such as subscriptions, bookings or shipping rules, and whose name is the licence in?
- Will they build on a staging copy and test checkout there before anything touches the live store?
- What happens to orders if an update breaks the checkout at night in your time zone?
- Can they show a live store they built, so you can add a product to the basket yourself?

If you are choosing between WooCommerce and another platform, our [website design and development](https://ndcompany.in/services/website-design-development) page explains how we recommend one in writing.

## Who owns the code in a WordPress project?

Often nobody says. In our sample, 8 agencies say the client owns the code or site after payment, 1 keeps custom code as its own unless it is transferred in writing, 1 is unclear and 20 say nothing. One more agency on the same list, based outside India, keeps the site and its intellectual property until all 12 monthly payments have been made. We mention it only to show that terms like that exist.

WordPress adds one wrinkle. WordPress's own licence page says [plugins and themes are derivative work and thus inherit the GPL license](https://wordpress.org/about/license/). In practice the question is not whether you may use the theme you paid for, but what you hold: a copy you can edit and keep, or only a licence that ends when the agency relationship does. A custom theme written for you and a premium theme the agency bought are different things, and so are a page builder's licence and a plugin's. Ask the agency which parts of your site are custom, which are licensed from someone else, and in whose name each licence is held. If this matters to your business, show the contract to a lawyer before you sign.

With us, the wording is that ownership of the final deliverables named in your quote passes to you on full payment. We list the deliverables in the quote, so nothing is left to assumption.

## Do WordPress developers in India have credentials?

None of the 30 claim a credential that is specific to WordPress, such as a place in a WordPress VIP partner listing or a record of contributing to WordPress itself. One says "certified WordPress developers" and does not say who certifies them. That does not make the developers weak. It means a claim on a website tells you very little, and what you can check is the work.

Ask to open two live sites they built on WordPress, and look at them on your phone. Ask which plugins they chose and why, whether they wrote any custom code, and who maintains the sites now. A developer who has plugins or themes of their own, or a public WordPress.org profile, can point you to them. If they cannot show you any work you can open, treat the claim as unproven.

## Will they work in your time zone, and will they sign an NDA?

10 of the 30 mention signing an NDA. The others may sign one too, so ask before you share anything confidential. 7 claim to work in or overlap your hours. The most specific says five business hours of overlap a day. India runs on India Standard Time all year, five and a half hours ahead of UTC, so a UK client has a four and a half to five and a half hour gap and a US client nine and a half to thirteen and a half. Agree in writing when you can have a call, how soon an email gets a reply and what a developer does when you report a fault out of hours. For a more general version of this question, see [how to choose an SEO agency in India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/choose-seo-agency-india), where we ran the same checks on SEO firms.

## What should you put in writing before you hire?

Almost everything you need is a short list. Put these in the contract or the signed quote.

1. Code and theme ownership. State which deliverables you own on full payment, and which parts are third-party and only licensed.
2. Admin access. You get an administrator account on the live site from day one, and the agency has its own named account, so you can remove it later.
3. Hosting and domain in your name. The hosting account and domain registration are yours, with the agency added as a user, never the other way round.
4. Plugin and theme licences in your name. List every paid plugin, theme and page builder, who pays and when each renews.
5. A staging site. Changes are made on a copy first, and you approve them before the live site changes.
6. Backups. Say how often, where they are stored, who can restore one and how fast.
7. A support period. Say how long fixes are free after launch, what counts as a fix and what is a new request. In our sample, 5 of 30 state one, from one to three months.
8. An NDA, signed before you share anything private.
9. Payment terms. The price and the stages are set in writing before work starts, along with who pays any payment charges.
10. Handover. What you receive at the end, such as a copy of the files and database, and the logins, and on what day.

Before you ask for quotes, write down what the site must do and which pages it needs. Our guide on [planning a business website](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/plan-a-business-website) covers that, and a clear brief is what makes two quotes comparable.
