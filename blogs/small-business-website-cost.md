# What does a small business website cost in 2026?

The build price, then the running costs: .com renewals, hosting after the introductory term, Shopify and card fees, business email and the ADA, with a two-year example in US dollars.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 25 October 2026 6 min read

A small business website costs a one-off build plus yearly running costs. With us the build starts at $149 for a landing page or small brochure site, $299 for a multi-page business site on WordPress or another CMS, and $449 for an online store or web application. After that you pay for the domain (about $11 to $23 a year for a .com), hosting (about $10 to $14 a month once the introductory price ends), email if you want it, and card fees of roughly 2.9% plus 30 cents on each online sale.

Key takeaways

- Build: $149, $299 or from $449 with us, quoted in writing before work starts. Our [pricing page](https://ndcompany.in/pricing) lists every service in US dollars.
- A .com costs about $11 a year at registrars that price it flat; $0.01 offers need a 3-year term and renew at $19.99 to $22.99.
- Introductory hosting prices need 36 to 48 months paid upfront and renew at two to four times the price.
- Stripe and Shopify Payments both charge 2.9% plus 30 cents on a standard online card payment.
- The ADA covers the websites of businesses open to the public. There is no detailed federal technical standard for them, and the Department of Justice points to WCAG as guidance.

## An online store

Shopify's plans are $29, $79 and $299 a month paid yearly (Basic, Grow and Advanced), according to Shopify's own [guide to e-commerce website costs](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-website-cost), or $39, $105 and $399 billed monthly ([Shopify on POS system costs](https://www.shopify.com/blog/pos-system-cost)). Shopify Payments charges 2.9% plus 30 cents per online card payment on Basic, 2.7% on Grow and 2.5% on Advanced ([Shopify on card processing fees](https://www.shopify.com/blog/credit-card-processing-fees)). Use another payment provider and Shopify adds 2%, 1% or 0.6% on top ([Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)).

WooCommerce on WordPress has no platform fee: "Our core platform is free" ([WooCommerce on WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/woocommerce/)). You pay for hosting that can carry a store, any paid extensions, and your payment provider's fees.

## The parts that are free

- **SSL certificate.** Let's Encrypt gives "a trusted certificate at zero cost" to anyone who owns a domain ([about Let's Encrypt](https://letsencrypt.org/about/)), and most hosts install one for you.
- **WordPress.** Free software, released under the GPLv2 or later ([WordPress licence](https://wordpress.org/about/license/)).

## Business email

Google Workspace Business Starter is $8.40 per user per month on the flexible plan, which you can cancel at any time ([Google Workspace flexible plan](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/flexible-plan)), or $7 per user per month with a one-year commitment, $84 a year ([Google's comparison of payment plans](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/compare-flexible-and-annual-fixed-term-payment-plans)).

## Accessibility is part of the job

The Department of Justice says "the ADA's requirements apply to all the goods, services, privileges, or activities offered by public accommodations, including those offered on the web." It also says it "does not have a regulation setting out detailed standards", that businesses "can currently choose how they will ensure" their sites are accessible, and it names the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines as existing technical standards that "provide helpful guidance" ([ADA.gov guidance on web accessibility](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)).

For state and local governments there is a fixed standard: WCAG 2.1 level AA, due by 26 April 2027 for larger entities and 26 April 2028 for smaller ones ([ADA.gov fact sheet on the Title II web rule](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/)). Put the level you want in your brief, so it is built in from the start rather than fixed afterwards.

## How to compare website quotes

- Check that every quote names the same pages, features and revision rounds.
- Ask whether hosting, the domain and email are included, for how long, and what they renew at.
- Ask for WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 level AA in writing.
- Ask who owns the domain, the accounts and the final files once you have paid.
- Get the price, the deliverables and the payment schedule in writing before you pay a deposit.
