# White label SEO explained: how agencies resell SEO safely

We read 16 white label SEO provider websites. 15 offer reports under your brand, 3 publish a monthly price and none says who keeps the client's Google accounts. How the model works and what to put in writing.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 11 November 2026 13 min read

White label SEO is search engine optimisation done by one company and sold by another under its own name. The agency keeps the client, sets the price and sends the reports with its own logo. The provider does the work and stays out of sight. It is safe when four things are written down: who talks to the client, who owns the client's Google accounts, how links are built and what happens when the work ends. On 11 November 2026 we read the websites of 16 white label SEO providers. 15 offer reports under the agency's brand, 3 publish a monthly price, 5 mention an NDA, and none says who keeps the client's Search Console, Analytics or Business Profile once the arrangement ends.

Key takeaways

- White label SEO, private label SEO and SEO reselling describe the same basic model: one firm does the SEO, another sells it as its own. No official body defines the terms, so read what each provider's contract says.
- 15 of the 16 provider sites we read offer unbranded or agency-branded reports or a dashboard. Only 5 mention signing an NDA.
- 3 of 16 publish a monthly price: $99 to $349 a month per website, $355 to $2,219 a month at one provider's member rate, and 1,300 to 2,500 pounds a month per client.
- 2 of 16 publish prices per link, from $175 to $450 at one and 80 to 2,500 pounds per placement at the other. Google's spam policies call buying links for ranking purposes link spam.
- 0 of 16 say who keeps the client's Google accounts when the work ends. Google's own rules say the business should stay the owner of its Business Profile at all times.

## How we did the study

We searched for white label SEO pricing and SEO reseller programs on 11 November 2026 and took the 20 providers whose own websites came up. Four of them refused automated reading of every page, so the study covers the 16 we could read. For each one we read only its own pages: the white label or reseller page, pricing, FAQ and terms. We recorded what each site says, with the page it came from. It records what providers say about themselves, not how good their work is, and we do not name them here.

## What is white label SEO?

White label SEO is an arrangement in which an SEO provider does search optimisation work for an agency's clients, and the agency sells that work as its own. The client signs with the agency and pays the agency. The agency pays the provider a wholesale price agreed between them and charges its client whatever it chooses. Everything the client sees, from the audit and the monthly report to any email about the work, carries the agency's name or no name at all. The provider never sells to the client directly and usually never speaks to it. The name comes from white label goods: a product made by one company and packaged under another's brand. For an agency, the appeal is that it can offer SEO next to web design, ads or social media without hiring SEO specialists. The risk is that the agency answers to its client for work it did not do itself, so it needs to know exactly how that work is done.

## Is private label SEO the same thing? What is an SEO reseller?

Mostly, yes. Private label SEO, SEO reselling and outsourced SEO fulfilment are all used for one firm doing the SEO and another selling it. Some providers draw lines between them, such as exclusivity or a fixed menu of packages, but there is no standard definition, so a label tells you less than the contract does.

A white label SEO program, or reseller program, is a provider's standing offer to agencies: a wholesale price list, a branded reporting dashboard, sometimes sales material, and an account manager. One provider in our sample sells a membership that sets the wholesale rate an agency pays. Another sells a platform at $99 to $999 a month, on a one-year contract for its paid plans, with SEO as one of the services on it. Ask which of these you are buying: a person doing work on your client's site, software that produces reports, or both.

## How does white label SEO work day to day?

The work itself is ordinary SEO. What changes is who does what, and whose name is on it.

**Who does what in a typical white label SEO arrangement**

| Task | Agency | Provider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells SEO and signs the client | Yes | No |
| Sets the client's price | Yes | No |
| Writes the brief and agrees the scope | Yes, with the client | Quotes against it |
| Audit, keyword research, on-page fixes, content, links | Reviews and approves | Does the work |
| Access to the client's Google accounts | Arranges it with the client | Works through the access it is given |
| Monthly report | Sends it to the client | Writes it, unbranded or with the agency's logo |
| Client calls and complaints | Handles them | Joins only if the agency asks |

Access is where most of the practical detail sits. In Google Search Console, [Google's help page on users and permissions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7687615) says owners can add and remove users and use every tool, a full user can view all data and take some actions, and a restricted user has simple view rights on most data. Only an owner can add users. So the client stays the owner, the agency gets the access it needs, and the provider is added as a full or restricted user, under an email address the agency chooses if the provider should not appear by name.

## Who talks to the client?

In most arrangements, only the agency. In our sample, 7 of the 16 providers say they never contact the agency's clients or that the client never learns they exist. 4 offer to speak to the client as part of the agency's team, on calls or through a support line, if the agency wants that. One of them says that if an agency's client approaches it directly, it declines the work and tells the agency. The rest do not say.

Either model can work if it is agreed in advance. Before the first client, settle three things in writing: whether the provider may ever speak to your client, under what name it introduces itself, and what it does if your client finds it and asks it to work directly. That last point is what a non-solicitation clause covers, in the next section.

## NDAs, non-solicitation and ownership: what goes in the contract?

Three clauses carry most of the protection in a white label deal. We explain what each one is for. This is general information, not legal advice, and a lawyer in your own country should read the contract before you sign.

### The NDA

A non-disclosure agreement stops the provider from sharing your client's name, data or results, and from showing the work in its portfolio. Only 5 of the 16 providers mention one on their sites. Others may still sign, so ask, and have it signed before you send the client's name or give anyone access to an account.

### The non-solicitation clause

This is the provider's promise not to approach your clients, or take work from them, for a set period. Without it, the safest white label provider is the one that never learns who your client is, which is not practical once it is working in the client's accounts. Ask how long the promise lasts after your last project, and whether it covers a client who approaches the provider first.

### Ownership of accounts and work

None of the 16 sites says who keeps the client's Search Console, Analytics or Business Profile when the arrangement ends. Four say deliverables become yours on payment, which covers reports and content, not accounts. Keep every account registered to the client, never to the provider. For local SEO there is a Google rule as well: [Google's Business Profile third-party policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7353941) tell anyone managing a profile for a business to make the business owner the owner of the profile and themselves the manager, and say the business must keep ownership or co-ownership at all times. The same policy requires the business owner's express consent before a third party claims or manages a profile, and bans guaranteeing top placement on Google.

## What are the risks of white label SEO?

The main risk is that your agency's name goes on work you cannot see being done. Google is clear about where that leaves you. Its page ["Do you need an SEO?"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) says: "Ultimately, you are responsible for the actions of any companies you hire." In a white label chain, your client hired you, so your client holds you responsible for what the provider does.

### Link spam

[Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) define link spam as creating links to or from a site primarily to manipulate search rankings. The examples include buying or selling links for ranking purposes, exchanging goods or services for links, links with optimised anchor text in guest posts and press releases on other sites, and using automated programs or services to create links. The same page says sites that break the policies may rank lower or not appear at all. Paid links are allowed when they are marked: Google's page on [qualifying outbound links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links) asks for paid placements to carry `rel="sponsored"`, with `rel="nofollow"` still accepted.

Two of our 16 providers publish prices per link, from $175 to $450 at one and from 80 to 2,500 pounds per placement at the other. One more bundles 25 to 100 backlinks a month into its general SEO packages. None of this is proof of anything wrong, because outreach and digital PR can earn real links. But a price per link or a monthly link count bought to raise rankings is what Google describes, and the client's site carries the result. Ask where links will come from and ask to see five built last month.

### Ranking guarantees

None of the 16 promises a ranking, and 6 say rankings cannot be guaranteed or disclaim any warranty of results. Google's hiring page says no one can guarantee a number one ranking on Google. One provider's general SEO packages, not its reseller offer, still charge a fee per keyword that reaches page one. If a provider promises you positions, do not pass that promise on to a client.

### Charging for Google's free products

If your fee includes managing a client's Business Profile, Google's third-party policy requires you to tell the client that Business Profile is a service provided at no extra cost, to disclose your management fee in writing before you start, and to show it on invoices. It also requires agencies that mainly serve small and medium businesses to link Google's disclosure notice from a clearly discoverable place on their website. A white label provider cannot do this for you, because the client relationship is yours.

## What does a monthly white label SEO deliverable list look like?

A useful list names the work and the evidence for it, so you can check one against the other before forwarding the report. Here is the list behind our own [white label SEO service](https://ndcompany.in/white-label/seo), as an example of what to expect from any provider.

- In the first month, an audit: a crawl of the client's site for indexing problems, broken links, redirect chains, slow templates and missing metadata, delivered as a fix list ranked by priority.
- Keyword research: the phrases the client's customers search for, grouped by intent and mapped to existing or new pages.
- On-page work on titles, meta descriptions, headings, internal links, image alt text and schema markup, updated page by page.
- New pages or posts written to answer each search directly, a set number each month.
- Local SEO where the client serves an area: Business Profile setup or cleanup and consistent business details across directories.
- Link building by outreach, plus reclaiming links the site has already earned, with the links named in the report.
- A monthly report with rankings, organic traffic, indexing status and a log of the work done, unbranded or with the agency's logo.

The log matters most. A report that shows rankings without saying what changed on the site leaves you unable to explain a fall to your client. For what an audit contains in detail, read [how our SEO audit works](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/free-seo-audit-how-it-works).

## How much does white label SEO cost?

Most providers do not say. 13 of the 16 give no monthly white label price on their sites and price by quote, by volume or by project. The three that publish one price in very different ways.

**White label SEO prices published on 3 of 16 provider websites, 11 November 2026**

| How it is priced | Published range | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per website, platform-led plans | $99 to $349 a month | Month to month |
| Package tiers, at one membership level | $355 to $2,219 a month | Month to month |
| Per client, managed by a team | 1,300 to 2,500 pounds a month | Rolling monthly, 30 days' notice in its terms |

Beyond monthly retainers, the pricing models we saw were per project, per link or placement, per audit (one provider lists 300 to 800 pounds for a technical audit) and membership fees that unlock wholesale rates. Three providers state a notice period: 15 days, 30 days and 48 hours before renewal.

The price you pay a provider and the price your client pays you are separate decisions. When you compare providers, compare what a month buys: hours or deliverables, the number of content pieces, how links are built and what the report contains. For what SEO costs when bought direct, see [what SEO costs in India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/seo-cost-india).

## What should you check before choosing a white label SEO provider?

Start with the questions Google suggests on its hiring page, such as asking for examples of previous work, whether the SEO follows Google Search Essentials, what results it expects and in what timeframe, and whether it will share every change it makes to the site. Then add the white label ones.

1. Will you sign our NDA before we share a client's name?
2. Will you ever contact our client, and under what name do you appear in their accounts and on calls?
3. How long does your promise not to work for our clients last, and does it cover a client who approaches you first?
4. Do the client's accounts stay in the client's name, with you added as a user?
5. Where do links come from? Show us five you built last month.
6. Do you promise rankings? (The right answer is no.)
7. Can we see a sample monthly report with a log of the work done?
8. What is in a month: deliverables, content count, hours?
9. What is the notice period, and what do we receive when we leave?
10. Who owns the deliverables on payment, and can we pass them to our client?

We ran similar checks on Indian SEO agencies that sell to clients directly in [how to choose an SEO agency in India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/choose-seo-agency-india), and the same warning signs apply here.
