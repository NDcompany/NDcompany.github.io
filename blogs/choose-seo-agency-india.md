# How to choose an SEO agency in India: a checklist for businesses abroad

The questions Google tells you to ask an SEO, the red flags it warns about, how to keep your Search Console in your name, and what Indian agencies charge.

By [Nakshatra Ranjan Saha](https://ndcompany.in/team/nakshatra-ranjan-saha), CEO & Founder Published 6 November 2026 6 min read

To choose an SEO agency in India, start with the questions Google itself tells site owners to ask an SEO: examples of past work, whether they follow Google Search Essentials, what results they expect and by when, and how they will tell you about every change. Then rule out anyone who guarantees rankings or sells links, keep your Google accounts in your own name, and get the monthly scope and the report in writing. Price and time zones matter too, but they come after those checks.

Key takeaways

- Google publishes [a list of questions to ask before you hire an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo). Use it as your interview script.
- Google says no one can guarantee a number one ranking. A guarantee, a "special relationship" with Google or a "priority submit" offer is a reason to walk away.
- Buying links for ranking purposes is link spam under [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). Ask every agency how it builds links.
- Stay the owner of your Search Console property and add the agency as a user, which you can remove at any time.
- Indian agencies that publish SEO prices charged a median of ₹25,000 a month across their packages in our October 2026 study.

## Why businesses abroad hire an SEO agency in India

The usual reasons are cost and coverage. Monthly SEO is mostly skilled hours: audits, fixes, research, writing and reporting. Agencies in India price those hours for their own market, and many work in English all day. The time difference also means work can move while your office is closed.

None of that says anything about quality, which varies as much in India as anywhere else. The rest of this guide is about telling a careful agency from a careless one, wherever it is based. If you are weighing outsourcing more broadly, our [guide to outsourcing web development to India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/outsource-web-development-india) covers contracts, payments and access in more detail.

## What should an SEO agency do for you?

Google's page on hiring an SEO lists the services a good one can provide: a review of your site's content or structure, technical advice on website development, content development, keyword research, SEO training, expertise in specific markets and geographies, and optimizing for generative AI. A monthly package usually mixes several of these.

In practice, the first month should produce an audit: a crawl of your site, a list of problems ranked by impact, and a plan. If an agency cannot show you an audit before it starts monthly work, you have no baseline to judge it against. You can see what a basic audit checks in our post on [how our free SEO audit works](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/free-seo-audit-how-it-works), and our [technical SEO checklist](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/technical-seo-checklist) covers the fixes most sites need first.

## Which questions should you ask before you hire?

Google suggests asking a potential SEO these questions, and they work well as an interview script for an agency in any country:

1. Can you show me examples of your previous work and share some success stories?
2. Do you follow the Google Search Essentials?
3. What kind of results do you expect to see, and in what timeframe? How do you measure your success?
4. What is your experience in my industry?
5. What is your experience in my country or city?
6. What is your experience developing international sites?
7. How long have you been in business?
8. How can I expect to communicate with you? Will you share with me all the changes you make to my site?

Google also suggests checking business references with past clients, and seeing whether the SEO is interested in you and your business. For a buyer abroad, question five matters most. An agency that has only ranked local businesses in Indian cities may not know your market, your competitors or how people in your country search.

## What are the red flags?

Google's own warnings cover most of them. It says that no one can guarantee a number one ranking on Google, and to beware of SEOs that claim to guarantee rankings, allege a special relationship with Google or advertise a priority submit to Google. Google also says it never accepts money to include or rank sites in its results.

- **Guaranteed positions.** Rankings depend on Google and on your competitors, so a guarantee is either untrue or tied to a phrase nobody searches.
- **Bought links.** Google's spam policies list buying or selling links for ranking purposes as link spam. Paid links are fine only when they are marked with `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`, which means they pass no ranking credit. Ask how the agency builds links, and ask to see some.
- **Links to the agency.** Google says you should never have to link to an SEO. A footer credit or a hidden link on your site should not be part of the deal.
- **Cold emails.** Google says to be wary of SEO firms that email you out of the blue. An unsolicited offer is not proof of a bad agency, but check it the same way you would check any other.
- **No written scope.** If nobody can say what gets done each month, you cannot tell whether it was done.

## Keep your accounts in your name

Your Search Console property, your analytics, your Google Business Profile and your website hosting should stay registered to your business. The agency gets access; it does not get ownership. If the work ends, you keep the data and the history.

Search Console makes this simple. [Google's help page on owners and users](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7687615) explains that a property owner adds people under Settings, Users and permissions, as a full user, who can view all data and take some actions, or a restricted user, who has view rights to most data. Only owners can add or remove users. Google's hiring page goes further for the early stage: give a new SEO read access only, not write access. If you ever remove an owner, also remove their verification token from your site, or they can verify again.

## What does an SEO agency in India cost?

On 29 October 2026 we read the websites of 108 Indian agencies. Fifteen of them published monthly SEO prices in rupees, 37 packages in all, with a median of ₹25,000 a month across the packages and ₹15,000 a month at each agency's cheapest tier. Our [study of SEO prices in India](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/seo-cost-india) has the full table and what drives a quote up or down.

Agencies that work with clients abroad often quote in US dollars, pounds or euros instead, at prices set for those markets. Compare quotes on scope rather than on the headline figure: how many pages get optimized, how many articles a month, what link building means in practice, and what the report shows.

## How does the time difference work?

India runs on India Standard Time all year, five and a half hours ahead of UTC. That puts it four and a half to five and a half hours ahead of the UK, depending on summer time, and nine and a half to thirteen and a half hours ahead of the US, depending on your coast and the season. For SEO that gap is rarely a problem, because most of the work does not need a call. A weekly or monthly call can sit in the overlap: early morning in the US, or late morning in the UK.

What matters more is how the agency reports. Ask for a written monthly report with rankings, organic traffic, indexing status and a log of the work done, so you can check progress without a meeting.

## What to put in writing

- The monthly scope: pages optimized, articles written, technical fixes and link building, in numbers where possible.
- What the monthly report contains and when it arrives.
- Who owns the content and the accounts, and how access is handed back if the work ends.
- The notice period and what happens to work in progress.
- The currency, the payment method and when invoices are due.
