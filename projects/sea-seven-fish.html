Overview

N&D Co. designed the brand identity for Sea Seven Fish, a seafood wholesale business from Northeast India. We presented 18 logo concepts, and the client chose Mountains & Waters: a fish whose dorsal fin is a row of mountain peaks, swimming over wave lines that stand for the region's rivers. The identity around it has four logo versions in four colourways, a blue palette of four colours, two fonts, a brand seal, two business card designs and a guidelines document.

The work covers three of our services: brand identity design for the system and its rules, logo design for the concepts and the final marks, and graphic design for the seal and the business cards. Our founder, Nakshatra Ranjan Saha, prepared the brand identity document, dated 2 July 2026.

The idea that runs through the identity is provenance. Many seafood brands use a similar fish icon, so the mark puts the place the business comes from into the fish itself: it says the products start among the mountains and rivers of Northeast India.

Sea Seven Fish uses the identity today. Photos of it in use are covered by a non-disclosure agreement, so this page shows the presentation mockups from the brand package instead.

About Sea Seven Fish

Sea Seven Fish is a seafood business from Northeast India, the region of the Seven Sister states, and our proposal was addressed to Sea Seven Fish Pvt. Ltd. Its business cards describe it as a North East supplier, and our brand documents treat it as a wholesale brand that connects suppliers with markets. The brand seal we designed reads ESTD 2026, and the primary logo carries the line Fresh from the sea.

The name gave the concepts most of their material: the sea, the number seven, and the seven states of the Northeast that many of the concepts refer to.

What the identity was designed to do

Our documents explain what the identity was designed to do. In wholesale markets, many brands rely on a similar fish icon, so a fish alone would not set Sea Seven Fish apart. The mark had to say where the business comes from, and it had to work at every size it would meet, from a favicon to a sign on a building.

  • Stand apart from the generic fish icons common in seafood wholesale.
  • Tell the story of the business's origin in Northeast India.
  • Stay legible at favicon size and on large formats such as signage and packaging.

Logo Design

18 logo concepts, and the one the client chose

We started with 18 concepts, each a black icon with a short note on the idea behind it. Some fused a fish with the letter S and the number 7, some used the seven states as seven fish, paths or segments, and others told a supply story with a compass, a fishing boat or an infinity sign. The client chose concept 13, Mountains & Waters, and we developed it into the final logo system.

The finished marks are a primary emblem (the fish inside a round badge reading Fresh from the sea and Sea Seven Fish), a secondary combination mark (the fish in a circle beside the name on three lines), a tertiary curved combination mark (the fish over the name set on a curve) and the fish on its own as a brand icon.

Why did Sea Seven Fish choose the Mountains & Waters logo?

Mountains & Waters was the one concept that put the whole story of Sea Seven Fish into a single shape. Several of the other concepts each carried one idea well, whether seafood, distribution, trust or the seven states, but this one joined them without adding parts.

The fish says what the business sells at a glance. Its dorsal fin is drawn as a row of mountain peaks, after the landscape of Northeast India, so the place is part of the fish itself and not a badge added beside it. The wave lines under the fish stand for the rivers and waterways that support the region's fishing communities and link the land to the seafood trade.

Read together, the logo says that the products start in a place of mountains and rivers before they reach customers across the country. So the logo is a mark of origin, and a competitor cannot easily copy where a business comes from.

How is the Sea Seven Fish logo constructed?

We built the Sea Seven Fish logo on geometry, not freehand drawing. A modular grid sets its proportions, alignment and clear space, so every part relates to the others by measurable ratios. Circles define the main curves of the fish, the badge and the spacing of the lettering, which keeps the transitions smooth and the shape consistent.

The composition follows an Archimedean spiral, in which the distance between each turn stays the same. A golden spiral grows exponentially, while an Archimedean one keeps an even rhythm, which suits a compact logo. The spiral is a guide, not something you can see in the finished mark: it controls the flow of the fish's outline, sets the relationship between the icon and the name and spreads the visual weight evenly.

Consistent stroke weights, equal optical spacing and proportional scaling do the rest. The result reads the same at favicon size as it does on a sign, and it reproduces the same way in print and on screen.

Rules for using the logo

The package's README says how to use the logo. Use the official files, keep enough clear space around the logo and pick the colour version that suits the background. Do not stretch, distort or rotate the logo, change its colours, add shadows, outlines or effects, change its proportions or redraw it by hand.
