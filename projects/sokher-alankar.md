# Sokher Alankar: a jewellery store on Blogger that costs nothing to run

A brand identity and a free online store on Blogger for Sokher Alankar, handmade Bengali jewellery by one maker: 42 products, orders on WhatsApp by product code, ₹0 a month to run.

## Overview

Sokher Alankar is handmade Bengali jewellery made by one woman, Adrija Ghosh, at home. N&D Co. gave it a brand identity and an online store that costs nothing a month to run: a custom theme on Google's free Blogger platform, 42 products in four collections, 25 journal posts, and an Order on WhatsApp button on every piece that opens a chat with the product code already typed. The store went live on 15 November 2026 at [sokheralankar.blogspot.com](https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/). Before it, Adrija got 10 to 12 enquiries a month; in its first five days she got 15 to 20.

Two facts shaped every decision. Adrija had no budget for a domain, hosting or a store plan, so the running cost had to be zero. And she runs the business alone, so she wanted every enquiry to reach her on WhatsApp, where she already talks to customers, carrying a code like SA-016 that tells her at once which piece the buyer means.

The work covers five of our services: [brand identity design](https://ndcompany.in/services/brand-identity-design), [website design and development](https://ndcompany.in/services/website-design-development), [SEO](https://ndcompany.in/services/seo-services), [content creation](https://ndcompany.in/services/content-creation) and [video production](https://ndcompany.in/services/video-production-editing) for a launch trailer. This page shows each step with screenshots, including the settings most Blogger stores get wrong.

## About Sokher Alankar

The name is Bengali. Shokh is something you do for the love of it, and alankar means jewellery, so Sokher Alankar is jewellery made for the love of it. Adrija designs and makes every piece herself: thread and fabric necklaces, braided choker sets with studs and bangles, jute and fabric bangles, and combos with matching earrings, many with cowries, jute or oxidised-finish settings and small bells.

Her store has no cart, account or checkout. Prices follow the cost of raw materials, so Adrija shares the current price in the WhatsApp chat, and buyers pay her by UPI: Google Pay, PhonePe or Paytm.

Sources: [Sokher Alankar: About Adrija Ghosh](https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/p/about.html), [Sokher Alankar: How to order](https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/p/how-to-order.html).

## What the store had to do

Most store builders answer a different question: how to take card payments from strangers at scale. Adrija's question was how one person can show 42 handmade pieces, look trustworthy, and turn interest into a conversation, without a monthly bill.

- Cost nothing a month: no paid domain, hosting, theme, plugin or store plan.
- Send every enquiry to WhatsApp, the app Adrija already uses with customers.
- Put a product code in every message, so she never has to guess which piece a buyer means.
- Be fast on phones and readable by Google, without a developer on call.
- Look like Bengal, not like a template.

[Website Design & Development](https://ndcompany.in/services/website-design-development)

### A custom Blogger theme that works as a store

Blogger is free and hosts on Google's own servers, and it lets you replace its theme with your own code. We wrote the theme from scratch and switched off Blogger's default stylesheet and scripts, so each page loads only what it uses. Each product is a Blogger post with labels: Shop, its collection and its code. The theme reads those labels and draws a product page, with a photo gallery, a Price on request note and the Order on WhatsApp button. Collections are label pages, and the journal is the Journal label.

The button is a plain WhatsApp click-to-chat link with the message already written, so it needs no app, plugin or API. The message holds the code, the piece's name and its address, and WhatsApp shows a preview of the page with its photo, so Adrija sees the piece the buyer is asking about before she reads the words.

- A custom Blogger theme written from scratch, with Blogger's default CSS and JavaScript switched off
- Home page, four collection pages, a shop page, About and How to order
- 42 product pages, each with a code from SA-001 and a six-slide gallery: the photo, four branded slides (what you get, details, how to order, care) and the full uncropped photo
- An Order on WhatsApp button with the code, name and link in the message
- Product photos hosted free through GitHub and jsDelivr

[SEO Services](https://ndcompany.in/services/seo-services)

### Search basics Blogger leaves out

Blogger's defaults hide a store from Google in small ways, and fixing them took settings, not money. We wrote a title and description for every page type in the theme, kept the product code labels out of the menu and out of the index, added breadcrumb structured data to products and article data to journal posts, and replaced the default robots.txt, which blocks the label pages that a store uses as its collections.

We left out Product structured data on purpose. Google's product rich results need a price, a review or a rating, and Sokher Alankar publishes none of them, so adding it would only produce errors.

- Titles and descriptions for the home page, collections, products, journal and pages
- BreadcrumbList on products and BlogPosting on journal posts
- A custom robots.txt that opens the collection pages to search engines
- Short product addresses that end in the code, such as /pink-black-thread-choker-set-sa-005
- Keyword research from Google autocomplete for the product names and guides

## How we built it, step by step

Here is the build in the order it happened, with the screens behind each step. A solo maker with a little patience can copy most of it.

1. Brand first. We finished the identity on 31 October 2026, before any page existed, so the store's colours, fonts and patterns came from the guide rather than a template.
2. A Blogger blog on the free blogspot.com address. Blogger can move to a custom domain later, so starting free closes no doors ([Blogger Help](https://support.google.com/blogger/answer/1233387?hl=en)).
3. A theme written from scratch, with b:css and b:js set to false so Blogger adds none of its own styling or scripts.
4. Product photos into a public GitHub repository, served through jsDelivr's free CDN, with every address pinned to a commit so a changed photo can never show an old copy. Blogger's API cannot upload images, so this replaced its media library.
5. Each product published as a post through the Blogger API, labelled Shop, its collection and its code, with a short address that ends in the code.
6. The WhatsApp button built from WhatsApp's own link format, with the message typed in for the buyer.
7. Search settings: titles and descriptions, structured data, a custom robots.txt and a sitemap entry for pages.
8. Speed work on phones: smaller hero images, fonts that never reflow the heading, and a gallery that holds its space while it loads.
9. Journal posts and the launch trailer, then a last check on every page with PageSpeed Insights.

## The WhatsApp button and the product code

WhatsApp documents a link that opens a chat with a number and a message already typed: wa.me, then the number with its country code, then ?text= and the message ([WhatsApp Help Center](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934/?locale=en_US)). On Sokher Alankar every product page builds that link from the post itself. This is the exact link behind the button on SA-005:

```plaintext
https://wa.me/918388042089?text=Hi%20Adrija%2C%20I%20would%20like%20to%20order%20SA-005%20Pink%20and%20black%20thread%20choker%20with%20stud%20earrings%20and%20bangle.%20Please%20share%20today%E2%80%99s%20price.%20https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/2026/11/pink-black-thread-choker-set-sa-005.html
```

The buyer sees the message ready to send: a greeting, the code, the piece's name, a request for today's price and the page's address. They can change it before sending. Adrija gets three ways to recognise the piece in one message: the code, the name and the link preview with its photo.

**Why each part of the message is there**

| Part | What it does for a one person business |
| --- | --- |
| The code (SA-005) | Identifies the piece in a word, even when the buyer edits the rest of the message. Adrija can search her chats for it later. |
| The name | Lets her answer without opening the store, and catches a wrong code. |
| The link | WhatsApp turns it into a preview with the photo, so she sees the piece in the chat. |
| Today's price | Asks the question she would ask first anyway, because prices follow material costs. |

WhatsApp's own catalogue feature has a product code field for the same reason ([WhatsApp blog](https://blog.whatsapp.com/introducing-catalogs-for-small-businesses)). The difference is that a website page can be found on Google and shared anywhere, while a catalogue lives inside the app.

## What it costs to run, and what it would cost elsewhere

The store's running cost is zero: Blogger and its blogspot.com address are free, GitHub hosts the photos in a public repository at no charge, jsDelivr says it is "completely free to use" with no bandwidth limits ([jsDelivr](https://www.jsdelivr.com/)), and the WhatsApp Business app is free to download ([WhatsApp Help Center](https://faq.whatsapp.com/641572844337957/?locale=en_US)). That is the cost to keep it online, not what the build cost.

The free route has real limits, and we told Adrija about them before we started. There is no checkout, so payment and delivery are arranged in the chat (Adrija's buyers pay by UPI, through Google Pay, PhonePe or Paytm). The address is a blogspot.com one until she buys a domain. And Blogger's phone redirect (it adds ?m=1 to addresses on phones) costs a fraction of a second on the first page a visitor opens, which no theme can remove.

## Settings that let Google see the collections

One setting matters more than any other on a Blogger store. Blogger's default robots.txt disallows /search, and on Blogger every label page lives under /search/label/. A store that uses labels as collections hides every collection from search engines without knowing it. We switched on a custom robots.txt that allows the label pages first:

```plaintext
User-agent: *
Allow: /search/label/
Disallow: /search
Allow: /

Sitemap: https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/sitemap.xml
Sitemap: https://sokheralankar.blogspot.com/sitemap-pages.xml
```

Three smaller fixes follow from how Blogger writes pages. Bengali text inside structured data is written as escaped characters, because Blogger turns some Bengali vowel signs into HTML entities there. Schema addresses use the canonical home page, because Blogger adds ?m=1 on phones. And search descriptions are written in the Blogger editor, since its API ignores them; until then the theme writes a description from each page's title.

## Would this work for a maker outside India?

Yes, with two conditions. WhatsApp's click-to-chat link takes any number in international format, and Blogger is free in every country it serves, so a jewellery maker in London, a baker in Dubai or a candle maker in Texas can copy the setup as it is. The first condition is that the buyers already use WhatsApp, which varies by country; the same button can open an email or another messenger instead. The second is that a free store suits made-to-order and small-batch sellers who want to talk to each buyer. A shop that needs automatic payments, stock counts and shipping labels has outgrown it.

We wrote the method up as a guide for any solo seller: [how to run a free online store that takes orders on WhatsApp](https://ndcompany.in/blogs/free-online-store-whatsapp-orders).

## Frequently asked questions

**What did N&D Co. make for Sokher Alankar?**

A brand identity built around its hand-lettered Bengali logo, and a free online store on Blogger with 42 products, 25 journal posts and an Order on WhatsApp button on every piece. We also wrote the product copy and made a 34 second launch trailer in 16:9 and 9:16.

**Why build the store on Blogger instead of Shopify or Wix?**

Because the owner had no budget for a domain, hosting or a store plan. Blogger is free, runs on Google's servers and accepts a custom theme, so the store costs nothing a month. Shopify Basic in India is ₹1,499 a month billed yearly, before payment fees.

**How does a customer order from Sokher Alankar?**

They open a piece and tap Order on WhatsApp. WhatsApp opens with a message that already holds the product code, the piece's name and its link, and Adrija replies with the current price and answers questions. Buyers pay her by UPI, through Google Pay, PhonePe or Paytm. There is no cart, account or checkout.

**Did the free store bring Sokher Alankar more enquiries?**

Yes, so far. Before the store, Adrija got 10 to 12 enquiries a month. In the store's first five days she got 15 to 20, by her own count. A launch week brings its own attention, so we will report the count again at 30 and 90 days.

**Why does every WhatsApp message carry a product code like SA-005?**

Adrija runs the business alone, so the code tells her which piece a buyer means at a glance, even if the buyer edits the rest of the message. She can also search her chats for a code later.

**Where are the product photos hosted, and what does that cost?**

In a public GitHub repository, served through jsDelivr's free CDN, with every address pinned to a commit so a changed photo never shows an old copy. Both are free. Blogger's API cannot upload images, so this replaced its media library.

**How fast is the Sokher Alankar store?**

On Google's PageSpeed Insights, on 21 November 2026, every page scored 100 for accessibility, best practices and SEO on phones and desktops, and 100 for performance on desktop. On phones the home page, collections, journal posts and How to order scored 100 for performance, and product pages and the About page 95 to 99. No page had any layout shift.

**Can the store move to its own domain later?**

Yes. Blogger supports custom domains, so Sokher Alankar can buy one and point it at the same blog when the budget allows, keeping its pages.

**Could a seller outside India use the same setup?**

Yes, if their buyers use WhatsApp. The click-to-chat link takes any number in international format and Blogger is free worldwide. It suits made-to-order and small-batch sellers who want to talk to each buyer, not shops that need automatic payments and stock control.
